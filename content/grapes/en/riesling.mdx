## Meet the grape

Riesling loves cool climates and delicately reflects differences between vineyards. **New oak is rarely used**, so the aromas of grape and terroir come through as they are. Thanks to high acidity, it stays refreshing even with sweetness left in, and it can age for a long time.

## How it tastes

- **Aromas**: green apple, lemon and white flowers when cool; peach, apricot and lime when warm; honey and dried fruit in noble-rot wines.
- **Aged aromas**: honey, toast, and **petrol**, which resembles kerosene.
- **Structure**: very high acidity, light to medium body. Alcohol ranges from the Mosel's low levels to the higher levels of dry wines from Alsace and Austria.

## Where it grows

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Mosel (Germany) | Slate slopes. Light, low in alcohol, often delicately sweet |
| Rheingau, Pfalz (Germany) | Fuller, with a larger share of dry (trocken) wines |
| Alsace (France) | Mostly dry, a powerful style |
| Wachau (Austria) | Dry, with concentrated fruit and minerality |
| Clare Valley, Eden Valley (Australia) | Dry, with vivid lime |

On German labels, **Kabinett → Spätlese → Auslese → Beerenauslese → Trockenbeerenauslese** indicates how ripe the grapes were at harvest, and there is also **Eiswein**, made from frozen grapes.

## With food

Off-dry Riesling is especially good with mildly spicy food. Try it with tteokbokki (spicy rice cakes), Korean sweet-and-spicy fried chicken or Thai food. Dry Riesling is good with grilled fish and pork dishes.
