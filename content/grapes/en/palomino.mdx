## Meet the grape

Palomino is the key grape for making Sherry in the **Jerez** area of southern Spain. With low acidity and neutral aromas it makes ordinary table wine, but that very neutrality lets it take on the aromas created by Sherry-making cleanly. It thrives in the white chalky soil called **albariza**.

## How it becomes Sherry

A dry base wine is made, its alcohol raised with grape spirit, and it is aged in the **solera** system, which links tiers of older and younger casks. The style depends on how it is aged.

| Style | Ageing | Aromas |
|---|---|---|
| Fino, Manzanilla | Aged under a film of yeast called **flor** | Almond, bread dough, salty. Pale |
| Amontillado | Flor ageing, then oxidative ageing | Between nuts and caramel and salty notes |
| Oloroso | Oxidative ageing from the start | Walnut, toffee, leather. Dark brown |

Sweet Cream Sherry is made by blending dry Sherry with a sweet wine such as Pedro Ximénez.

## With food

Chilled Fino and Manzanilla go well with olives, almonds, jamón and fried seafood, and their salty umami also suits Korean side dishes such as crispy seaweed chips or stir-fried anchovies. Oloroso is great with nuts, aged cheese and rich meat stews.
