## Meet the grape

Chenin Blanc comes from Anjou and Touraine in the middle Loire. Its **high acidity** and thin skins, which are readily affected by **noble rot**, let one grape make almost every style. Today the country growing the most is **South Africa**, where it was long called "Steen".

## How it tastes

- **Aromas**: green apple, lemon and pear when cool; quince, peach and pineapple when ripe; honey and dried fruit in noble-rot wines.
- **Aged aromas**: honey, nuts, beeswax.
- **Structure**: high acidity. Body ranges from light to full depending on style.

## Where it grows

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Vouvray (Loire) | Dry (sec), off-dry (demi-sec), sweet (moelleux), sparkling |
| Savennières (Loire) | Intense, age-worthy dry wine |
| Coteaux du Layon, Bonnezeaux, Quarts de Chaume (Loire) | Noble-rot sweet wines |
| Saumur, Crémant de Loire | Traditional-method sparkling |
| Stellenbosch, Swartland (South Africa) | From zesty everyday wines to rich old-vine wines |

## With food

Dry Chenin suits pork, chicken and fish in cream sauce; off-dry suits mildly spicy food; and noble-rot sweet styles suit fruit tarts or blue cheese.
