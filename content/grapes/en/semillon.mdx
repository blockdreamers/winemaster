## Meet the grape

Semillon has fairly subtle aromas but good **texture and weight**. It shines more when **blended with Sauvignon Blanc** than on its own: Sauvignon Blanc brings acidity and aroma, while Semillon brings soft body and ageing potential. Its thin skins, which are readily affected by **noble rot (Botrytis)**, are another important trait.

## Three faces

| Style | Character | Signature regions |
|---|---|---|
| Dry blend | Blended with Sauvignon Blanc: lemon, grass, beeswax. Toast from oak ageing | Bordeaux (Pessac-Léognan, Entre-Deux-Mers) |
| Noble-rot sweet | Honey, apricot, dried fruit, orange marmalade. Ages for a long time | Sauternes, Barsac |
| Dry single-variety | Picked early, low in alcohol and high in acid. Toast and honey with age | Hunter Valley, Australia |

## The magic of ageing

Hunter Valley Semillon is a light, sharp wine with lemon and grassy notes when young, but after long ageing in bottle it transforms into a rich wine with **toast and honey**, even though no oak was used. Sauternes, too, can age for decades.

## With food

Dry white Bordeaux goes well with grilled fish, seafood and chicken, and Sauternes with foie gras, blue cheese (Roquefort) and fruit desserts.
