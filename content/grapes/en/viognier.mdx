## Meet the grape

Viognier comes from **Condrieu**, a small appellation in the Northern Rhône. Its aromas only bloom when it is fully ripe, but by then sugar has risen, so alcohol tends to be high and acid low. That's why it's considered a grape with tricky harvest timing.

## How it tastes

- **Aromas**: **apricot**, peach, and white flowers like honeysuckle.
- **Structure**: low to medium acidity, full body, high alcohol. A slightly oily texture.
- **Winemaking**: often made without oak flavour to preserve its aromas; top Condrieu sometimes gains texture from old oak or lees ageing. Most is best drunk young.

## Where it grows

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Condrieu (Northern Rhône) | 100% Viognier. Intense apricot and floral aromas |
| Côte-Rôtie (Northern Rhône) | Co-fermented in small amounts with Syrah to lift aroma and colour |
| Languedoc (southern France) | Easy-going single-variety wines |
| California, Australia | Single-variety, Shiraz-Viognier blends |

## With food

Its apricot notes and richness suit chicken in cream sauce, lobster, and mildly spicy curry.
