## Meet the grape

Chardonnay is an adaptable grape that grows well almost anywhere. Its own varietal aromas are not strong, so **climate** and **the winemaker's choices** shape its flavour enormously. That's why it's called "the winemaker's grape".

## Different faces in different climates

| Climate | Aromas | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Cool | Green apple, lemon, wet stone | Chablis |
| Moderate | Peach, melon, citrus | Côte de Beaune |
| Warm | Pineapple, mango, banana | Napa Valley, Australia |

## What winemaking adds

- **Oak ageing**: vanilla, toast, clove.
- **Malolactic fermentation**: turns sharp malic acid into softer lactic acid, giving a **buttery** flavour.
- **Lees ageing**: resting on spent yeast adds biscuity richness and a creamy texture.

These techniques are covered in detail in Chapter 12, "Oak and Ageing".
