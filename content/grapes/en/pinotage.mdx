## Meet the grape

Pinotage was created in the 1920s by Professor **Abraham Perold** of Stellenbosch University, who crossed **Pinot Noir** with **Cinsault**. South Africans called Cinsault "Hermitage" at the time, so the two names were combined into Pinotage. The aim was to combine Pinot Noir's finesse with Cinsault's hardiness.

## How it tastes

- **Aromas**: black fruit like blackberry and plum, with smoke, coffee and earth. Sometimes a banana-like note too.
- **Structure**: deep colour, medium acidity and tannin, generous body.
- **Range of styles**: from light, fruit-driven wines to rich, heavy oak-aged ones. There is even "coffee Pinotage", which plays up the coffee notes.

## Where it grows

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Stellenbosch | The most serious Pinotage. Oak-aged, with ageing potential |
| Across South Africa | Single-variety or "Cape blends" (blends including Pinotage) |

## With food

Its smoky notes suit charcoal-grilled meat and barbecue. It's often paired with braai, South African barbecue.
