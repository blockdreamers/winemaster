## Meet the grape

Grenache thrives in **hot, dry, windy places**. Its sturdy wood means old vines are often grown as free-standing bushes. Sugar builds up easily, making **high-alcohol** wines, and its thin skins mean the colour is paler than you might expect. In Spain it is called **Garnacha**, and in Sardinia, Italy, **Cannonau**.

## How it tastes

- **Aromas**: red fruit like strawberry, raspberry and cherry jam. White pepper and dried herbs (the garrigue of southern France).
- **Structure**: low acidity, medium-minus tannin, high alcohol and generous body.
- **With age**: leather, toffee and dried fruit.

## Where it grows

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Southern Rhône (Châteauneuf-du-Pape and others) | Blended with Syrah and Mourvèdre. Warm and full of spice |
| Spain (Priorat, Rioja and others) | Dense, powerful reds, blended with Tempranillo |
| Barossa Valley, Australia | Rich fruit from old vines, GSM blends |
| Provence, Tavel | A key grape for rosé |

## With food

With generous fruit and soft tannins, it goes well with grilled meat, stews, and sweet-and-savoury marinated dishes like Korean galbi.
