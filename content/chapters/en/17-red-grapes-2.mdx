<Section id="ladder" title="The temperature ladder">

The three Bordeaux brothers in chapter 16, "International red grapes I", share out roles within a blend. The three grapes in this chapter each take the lead on their own, with distinct personalities. Line them up by temperature and they are easy to remember.

| Grape | Preferred climate | Character in a line |
|---|---|---|
| **Pinot Noir** | Cool | Transparent, delicate red fruit |
| **Syrah / Shiraz** | Moderate to warm | Black fruit and pepper, a firm frame |
| **Grenache** | Hot and dry | Sun-ripened red fruit, high alcohol |

> At a Korean barbecue, Pinot Noir is boiled pork, Syrah is charcoal-grilled beef ribs, Grenache is sweet-marinated pork ribs. Different weight, different direction of flavour.

</Section>

<Section id="pinot-noir" title="Pinot Noir: transparent delicacy">

Pinot Noir comes from Burgundy and has **thin skins** and tight bunches. That makes it prone to fungal disease, and its early budding leaves it exposed to spring frost. Too hot and its aromas turn muddled; too cold and it fails to ripen. It is demanding, but ripened properly somewhere cool, it shows differences between vineyards more transparently than any other grape.

**Structure**: pale colour, low to medium tannin, **high acidity**, light to medium body.

| Climate | Aromas | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Cool | Cranberry, red cherry, strawberry | Burgundy in cool years, Germany |
| Moderate | Raspberry, red cherry, earthy notes | Côte de Nuits, Oregon |
| Warm | Black cherry, ripe plum, rich fruit | Parts of California and Australia |

With age, aromas of **mushroom, wet leaves and forest floor** appear.

**Winemaking**: protecting the delicate fruit is the key, so new oak is used with care. Some winemakers keep the grapes cold before fermentation to draw out colour and fruit (**cold soak**), and some ferment whole bunches with their stems (**whole bunch**) to add aroma and structure. In Champagne, the skins are removed immediately and it becomes a base for white wine.

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Côte de Nuits, Côte de Beaune (Burgundy) | Earth and red fruit, a different character in each vineyard |
| Champagne | A key Champagne grape. Blanc de Noirs |
| Germany (Spätburgunder) | From delicate to rich styles |
| Willamette Valley (Oregon) | Red fruit and freshness |
| Sonoma (Russian River Valley and others), Carneros (California) | Riper fruit, softer texture |
| Central Otago, Marlborough (New Zealand) | Vivid fruit, vibrancy |
| Yarra Valley, Mornington Peninsula (Australia) | Elegant cool-region style |

**Food**: with high acidity and soft tannins, it is wonderfully food-friendly. Duck, salmon, mushrooms and chicken, and Korean dishes such as bulgogi or japchae (glass noodles stir-fried with vegetables).

</Section>

<Section id="syrah" title="Syrah / Shiraz: pepper and black fruit">

Syrah comes from the northern Rhône and has **thick skins and deep colour**. The same grape is called Syrah in France and **Shiraz** in Australia, and the name can hint at the style. A label saying Syrah often means a cooler, restrained style; Shiraz often means a rich, full one.

**Structure**: deep colour, medium to high tannin, medium to high acidity, medium to full body.

| Climate | Aromas | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Moderate (on the cool side) | Blackberry, plum, **black pepper**, violet, smoked meat | Northern Rhône |
| Warm | Blackberry jam, liquorice, chocolate, sweet spice | Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale |

**Winemaking**: oak ageing is the norm. The northern Rhône uses a lot of old or large French oak, while rich Australian Shiraz traditionally used **American oak**, sometimes adding coconut and vanilla. In **Côte-Rôtie** in the northern Rhône, a little of the white grape **Viognier** may be co-fermented to lift the aromas and stabilise the colour. Australia's Shiraz-Viognier follows the same idea.

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Hermitage, Côte-Rôtie, Cornas (northern Rhône) | Pepper, violet, firm structure. Long ageing |
| Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph (northern Rhône) | The same character, lighter and more approachable |
| Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale (Australia) | Lush black fruit, chocolate, high alcohol |
| Hunter Valley (Australia) | Medium body, earthy, ages well |
| Southern Rhône, Languedoc | Blended with Grenache |
| Chile, South Africa, Hawke's Bay in New Zealand, Washington | Pepper on the cool side, fruit on the warm side |

**Food**: charcoal-grilled meat, barbecue, lamb, a steak with plenty of black pepper. At a Korean table, try it with charcoal-grilled ribs or lamb skewers.

</Section>

<Section id="grenache" title="Grenache: sun-soaked fruit">

Grenache is thought to come from north-east Spain, where it is called **Garnacha**. It **ripens late and copes well with drought and heat**, so it shines in hot, dry Mediterranean climates. It is often grown as a low, wind-resistant **bush vine**, and old vines decades old can give concentrated wine.

**Structure**: **thin skins and fairly pale colour**, low to medium tannin, low to medium acidity, and **high alcohol** from its high sugar.

**Aromas**: ripe red fruit such as strawberry, raspberry and red cherry, white pepper, liquorice and dried herbs. With age come dried fruit, toffee and leather.

**Winemaking**: **blending** is common to make up for its lack of acid, tannin and colour. The classic is **GSM** (Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre). Many producers use old or large oak, or concrete tanks, to protect the fruit. Pale and fruity, it is also excellent for **rosé**.

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Châteauneuf-du-Pape, Gigondas, Côtes du Rhône (southern Rhône) | The heart of the blend. Red fruit, herbs, warming alcohol |
| Tavel (southern Rhône) | Deep-coloured rosé |
| Priorat (Spain) | Old vines, intense and concentrated reds |
| Rioja, Navarra (Spain) | Blended with Tempranillo, rosé |
| Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale (Australia) | Old bush vines, GSM blends |
| Sardinia (Italy, as Cannonau) | Rich Mediterranean reds |
| Banyuls, Maury (southern France) | Fortified sweet wines |

**Food**: herb-seasoned lamb, Mediterranean stews, meat in tomato sauce. At a Korean table, it sits comfortably with sweet and spicy meat dishes such as marinated pork ribs or jeyuk bokkeum (spicy stir-fried pork).

</Section>

<Section id="recap" title="Key points">

- **Pinot Noir**: a thin-skinned, demanding cool-climate grape. Pale colour, high acidity, low tannin, **red fruit**, and with age **mushroom and forest floor**. Burgundy, Champagne, Oregon, Central Otago.
- **Syrah / Shiraz**: the same grape. Deep colour and firm structure. The northern Rhône shows **black pepper and violet**, Australia's Barossa **blackberry jam and chocolate**.
- In Côte-Rôtie, a little **Viognier** may be co-fermented with Syrah.
- **Grenache / Garnacha**: a grape of hot, dry places. Pale colour, **high alcohol**, ripe red fruit. The lead in the southern Rhône and Spain, in **GSM** blends and in rosé.
- Remember the three by the ladder **cool → warm → hot**.
