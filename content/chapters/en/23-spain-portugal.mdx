<Section id="iberia" title="A climate map of the Iberian peninsula">

The Iberian peninsula, home to Spain and Portugal, is more varied than you might think. At its centre lies a wide plateau, the **Meseta**, with hot summers and cold winters. The north-west Atlantic coast, by contrast, is so rainy and green it is called "Green Spain". The south and east are hot and dry, Mediterranean.

These differences carry straight through to wine style. All three climates from chapter 9, "Climate and terroir", fit within one peninsula.

| Climate | Region | Signature wines |
|---|---|---|
| Cool, damp Atlantic | Galicia in Spain, north-west Portugal | Rías Baixas Albariño, Vinho Verde |
| Continental plateau | Rioja, Ribera del Duero, Douro | Tempranillo reds, reds from Portuguese native grapes |
| Hot, dry Mediterranean | Catalonia, Andalusia, Alentejo | Priorat, Cava, Sherry, Alentejo reds |

As we saw in chapter 13, "Reading a label", Spain's quality categories run from **DOCa** (DOQ in Catalan; only Rioja and Priorat) to **DO** to the regional **Vino de la Tierra**. There is also **Vino de Pago** for single estates.

</Section>

<Section id="rioja" title="Rioja: the language of oak and ageing">

**Rioja** is Spain's flagship region, spread along the Ebro river in northern Spain. Mountains to the north shelter it from cold Atlantic winds, and to the east the warm Mediterranean influence grows. It is divided into three zones.

| Zone | Climate · soil | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Rioja Alta | West, high and cool. Clay-limestone | Fresh, elegant wines for ageing |
| Rioja Alavesa | North, cool. Limestone | Delicate, fragrant wines |
| Rioja Oriental (formerly Baja) | East, warm and dry | More Garnacha, richer wines |

The star is **Tempranillo**, which we met in chapter 18, "Regional signature grapes". It is blended with **Garnacha** (Grenache) for fruit and alcohol, and **Graciano** and **Mazuelo** (Carignan) for colour and acid. Whites are based on **Viura**.

The key to understanding Rioja is its **ageing categories**. A small sticker or statement on the back label tells you how long the wine was aged (for reds).

| Category | Minimum ageing | Tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Joven or no statement | No requirement | Fresh fruit with little oak |
| Crianza | 2 years (at least 1 in barrel) | Balance of fruit and oak |
| Reserva | 3 years (at least 1 in barrel) | Softer, with mature aromas starting to appear |
| Gran Reserva | 5 years (at least 2 in barrel) | Clear mature aromas of leather, tobacco and dried fruit |

Traditionally, Rioja was famous for long ageing in 225-litre **American oak barrels**, giving aromas of **vanilla, coconut and dill**. The differences between oak types from chapter 12, "Oak and ageing", show plainly here. Today there are also many modern styles using French oak, or shorter ageing that keeps the fruit, and new labelling that highlights village or single-vineyard names.

> "Crianza, Reserva, Gran Reserva" on a Rioja label is not a quality score but a clock telling you how long the wine waited.

</Section>

<Section id="spain-more" title="Plateau and slate: Ribera del Duero and Priorat">

**Ribera del Duero** lies north of Madrid along the Duero river, on a **plateau at around 700 to 1,000 m**. Summer days are hot but nights drop sharply, and spring frosts are common: an **extreme continental** climate. Its big **diurnal range** lets grapes ripen well by day and keep their acid at night. Here Tempranillo is called **Tinto Fino** or **Tinta del País**, and it gives wines with thicker skins and deeper colour and tannin than in Rioja. Vega Sicilia, often named Spain's greatest wine, is here.

**Priorat** is a small region in the mountain valleys of Catalonia. Its vineyards are steep and covered in **llicorella**, crumbling black slate. On this poor soil, old **Garnacha** and **Cariñena** (Carignan) vines bear tiny crops, giving intense, high-alcohol wines with a strong mineral feel. With Rioja, it is one of only two top-category (DOQ) regions in Spain.

| Region | Climate | Main grapes | Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rioja | Continental (Atlantic and Mediterranean influences) | Tempranillo, Garnacha | Reds varying with ageing category |
| Ribera del Duero | Extreme continental plateau | Tinto Fino (Tempranillo) | Rich, structured reds |
| Priorat | Warm Mediterranean, steep mountains | Garnacha, Cariñena | Concentrated, powerful reds |
| Rías Baixas | Cool, rainy Atlantic | Albariño | Crisp dry whites |
| Penedès (Cava) | Mediterranean | Macabeo, Xarel·lo, Parellada | Traditional-method sparkling |
| Jerez | Hot and dry, Atlantic breezes | Palomino | Sherry (fortified) |

<Section id="spain-coast" title="Seaside Spain: Albariño, Cava and Sherry">

**Rías Baixas** in north-west Galicia faces the Atlantic directly, so it is rainy and cool. Because of the humidity, vines are sometimes trained high on **pergolas** for good air flow. The grape is **Albariño**: peach, citrus and floral aromas, high acidity and a slightly salty feel that makes it great with seafood.

**Cava** is a sparkling wine made by the **traditional method**, with the second fermentation in the bottle, like Champagne. Most is made in **Penedès** near Barcelona, mainly from the native grapes **Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada**. Even basic Cava ages at least 9 months on its lees. Grown somewhere warmer than Champagne, its fruit is rounder, and prices are fair.

**Jerez** in southern Andalusia is the home of the fortified wine **Sherry**. It is made in the "Sherry triangle" linking the towns of Jerez de la Frontera, Sanlúcar de Barrameda and El Puerto de Santa María. **Palomino** grows on **albariza**, a white chalky soil that soaks up winter rain and releases it slowly. Sherry's secrets, such as the layer of yeast called **flor** covering the wine and the **solera** system that blends wines of many years little by little, are covered in detail in chapter 29, "Sparkling, sweet and fortified".

</Section>

<Section id="portugal" title="Portugal: Douro, Vinho Verde, Alentejo">

Portugal is a small country, but by sticking to **native grapes** rather than international ones, it has a strong identity.

The **Douro** is the valley where Spain's Duero continues across the border. Hand-built terraces run endlessly up steep **schist slopes** on both sides of the river, and summers are very hot and dry. Roots burrow deep through cracks in the barren schist in search of water. This valley has one of the oldest demarcated wine regions in the world, and it is the original home of **Port**.

Port is a **fortified wine**, as we learned in chapter 1. **Grape spirit** is added to red wine during fermentation, while plenty of sugar remains. As the alcohol rises, the yeast stops working and the grape's sweetness stays. Colour and tannin must be extracted as much as possible in a short time, so the grapes were traditionally trodden by foot in stone tanks (lagares). The wine was then taken to **Vila Nova de Gaia** at the river mouth to age. Styles such as ruby, tawny and vintage Port are covered in chapter 29.

Today the Douro is also highly praised for **dry red table wines** made from the same grapes. The main ones are **Touriga Nacional**, Touriga Franca, **Tinta Roriz** (= Tempranillo), Tinta Barroca and Tinto Cão. They are marked by deep colour, black fruit and floral aromas, and firm tannin.

| Region | Climate | Main grapes | Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Douro | Hot, dry continental valley | Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz | Port, rich dry reds |
| Vinho Verde | Cool, rainy Atlantic | Alvarinho, Loureiro | Light, crisp whites, often lightly spritzy |
| Alentejo | Hot and dry, plains | Aragonez (= Tempranillo), Trincadeira, Alicante Bouschet | Soft reds with ripe fruit |

**Vinho Verde** is the wine of the Minho region in the north-west. The name "green wine" refers not to colour but to something closer to "young, fresh". Thanks to Atlantic rain and coolness, it is mainly low-alcohol, high-acid whites with lemon and green apple. Lying just south of Spain's Rías Baixas, it grows the same grape, **Alvarinho** (Albariño).

The southern **Alentejo** is a hot, dry region of wide plains and cork oak forests. Many of its reds are soft with ripe fruit, and an ancient tradition of making wine in large clay jars called **talhas** lives on.

</Section>

<Section id="recap" title="Key points">

- Iberia has **Atlantic** (Galicia, Vinho Verde), **continental plateau** (Rioja, Ribera, Douro) and **Mediterranean** (Catalonia, Andalusia, Alentejo) climates.
- **Rioja** is a Tempranillo-led blend. Its ageing categories run **Crianza (2 years) → Reserva (3 years) → Gran Reserva (5 years)**, traditionally in **American oak**.
- **Ribera del Duero** makes rich **Tinto Fino** on a plateau with a big diurnal range; **Priorat** makes concentrated Garnacha and Cariñena on **llicorella** slate.
- **Rías Baixas** means Atlantic **Albariño**, **Cava** is traditional-method sparkling, and **Jerez** is Sherry from **Palomino** on albariza soil.
- The **Douro** means **Port** and dry reds (Touriga Nacional) from schist terraces, **Vinho Verde** light, crisp whites, and the **Alentejo** soft reds from the warm south.
