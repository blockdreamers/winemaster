<Section id="local-heroes" title="Grapes you have to visit">

The international grapes in chapters 14 to 17 grow all over the world. But some grapes only show their true worth on particular soil. Italy has hundreds of native grapes, and so do Spain and Portugal. And some grapes became more famous in a **new home** than in their original one.

| Grape | Signature country | Character in a line |
|---|---|---|
| **Sangiovese** | Tuscany, Italy | High acidity, cherry and herbs |
| **Nebbiolo** | Piedmont, Italy | Pale colour, intense tannin, roses and tar |
| **Tempranillo** | Spain | Red fruit in harmony with oak ageing |
| **Malbec** | Argentina (originally France) | Deep purple, soft black fruit |
| **Zinfandel / Primitivo** | USA, Italy | Jammy fruit, high alcohol |
| **Pinotage** | South Africa | A cross born in South Africa |

> International grapes are the world's common language; regional grapes are dialects. Know the dialect and you know the place.

</Section>

<Section id="sangiovese" title="Sangiovese: the heart of Tuscany">

Sangiovese is Italy's most planted red grape and the star of Tuscany. It ripens fairly late, so it thrives on warm, sunny slopes.

**Structure**: medium colour, **high acidity**, medium to high tannin, medium to full body.

**Aromas**: **red cherry**, red plum, **tomato leaf** and **dried herbs** such as oregano. With age, leather, earth and dried cherry. In cool years acid and herbs lead; in warm years, rich cherry fruit.

**Winemaking**: traditionally aged in large, old oak casks (**botti**) to keep the fruit and acid, while some modern producers use small French barriques to add vanilla and a softer texture. **Super Tuscans**, blended with Cabernet Sauvignon or Merlot, are famous too.

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Chianti, Chianti Classico | Mostly Sangiovese (at least 80% for Chianti Classico). Cherry, herbs, lively acidity |
| Brunello di Montalcino | 100% Sangiovese. Richer and more powerful, with long ageing before release (about five years after harvest) |
| Vino Nobile di Montepulciano | Mostly Sangiovese. Not to be confused with the Montepulciano grape |
| Toscana IGT (Super Tuscans) | Blended with international grapes, or Sangiovese alone |

**Food**: its high acidity makes it a perfect match for **tomato sauce**. Tomato pasta, pizza, ragù, Florentine T-bone steak, and even the pork in a tangy kimchi stew.

</Section>

<Section id="nebbiolo" title="Nebbiolo: pale colour, great power">

Nebbiolo is the grape of **Piedmont** in north-west Italy, and its name most likely comes from "nebbia", the autumn fog. It buds early and ripens very late, so it must be planted on the best south-facing slopes to ripen properly. A demanding grape, it rarely succeeds away from home.

**Structure**: **pale colour** (garnet even when young, quickly turning brick), **very high tannin**, **high acidity**, high alcohol, full body. It is easy to judge it a light wine by its colour and then be startled by the tannin.

**Aromas**: **roses**, violets, red cherry, dried herbs and **tar**. With age, leather, **truffle** and dried fruit. "Roses and tar" sums up Nebbiolo best.

**Winemaking**: it is aged a long time to tame its strong tannins. Traditionalists using large old casks and modernists using small new barrels argued for years, and today many producers take a middle path.

| Region | Style | Minimum ageing |
|---|---|---|
| Barolo | The most powerful, longest-lived Nebbiolo | 38 months (including 18 in oak) |
| Barbaresco | Elegant, softening a little sooner | 26 months (including 9 in oak) |
| Langhe Nebbiolo | Lighter, good for earlier drinking | Short requirements |
| Gattinara, Ghemme (northern Piedmont), Valtellina (Lombardy) | Cooler, more delicate styles | Rules vary by region |

**Food**: strong tannin needs rich meat. Brasato (beef braised in wine), truffle dishes, aged cheese, and long-braised dishes like galbi-jjim (Korean braised short ribs).

</Section>

<Section id="tempranillo" title="Tempranillo: the face of Spain">

Tempranillo is Spain's signature red grape. Its name comes from **temprano**, "early", and it does ripen earlier than other Spanish grapes. Its thick skins give fairly deep colour.

**Structure**: medium to deep colour, medium acidity, medium to high tannin, medium to full body.

**Aromas**: when young, fruit such as **red cherry, strawberry and plum**. Tempranillo takes particularly well to oak: **American oak** adds **coconut and vanilla**, French oak clove and toast. With long ageing come **leather, tobacco and dried fig**.

**Rioja ageing categories** (for reds)

| Category | Minimum ageing | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Joven and other unlabelled wines | No requirement | Fruit-driven, for early drinking |
| Crianza | 2 years (at least 1 in oak) | Balance of fruit and oak |
| Reserva | 3 years (at least 1 in oak) | Deeper mature aromas |
| Gran Reserva | 5 years (at least 2 in oak) | Leather, dried fruit, a delicate texture |

| Region | Other name | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Rioja (Spain) | Tempranillo | Blended with Garnacha and others. Elegant and rich in mature aromas |
| Ribera del Duero (Spain) | Tinto Fino | High altitude with a big diurnal range. Richer and more powerful |
| Toro (Spain) | Tinta de Toro | Very rich and robust |
| Douro (Portugal) | Tinta Roriz | A blending grape for Port and dry reds |

**Food**: roast lamb, jamón, chorizo, Manchego cheese. At a Korean table, try charcoal-grilled marinated pork or neobiani (thin marinated beef patties). We meet the regions again in chapter 23, "Spain and Portugal".

</Section>

<Section id="new-homes" title="Grapes that blossomed in new homes: Malbec, Zinfandel, Pinotage">

**Malbec**'s original home is **Cahors** in south-west France. Cahors Malbec was so dark it earned the nickname "black wine". But the place that made Malbec world famous today is **Mendoza, Argentina**.

- **Structure**: deep purple, medium to high tannin, medium acidity, full body.
- **Aromas**: blackberry, plum, black cherry, **violet**. Oak adds chocolate and vanilla. Cahors tends to be firmer and earthier; Argentina softer and fruitier.
- **Regions**: Mendoza's vineyards sit at **high altitude** in the Andean foothills (around 1,000 m above sea level and higher), where strong sunshine builds colour and fruit and cool nights keep the acidity. Luján de Cuyo and the Uco Valley are famous.
- **Food**: Argentine charcoal-grilled beef (asado) is the classic match. Korean grilled short ribs work too.

**Zinfandel** is California's signature grape, but DNA analysis revealed it is the same grape as **Primitivo** from Puglia in Italy, with roots in **Croatia**.

- **Features**: berries within one bunch ripen unevenly, so raisined berries and unripe ones can sit side by side. Waiting for the whole bunch to ripen pushes sugar up, so **alcohol tends to run high**.
- **Aromas**: blackberry jam, blueberry, raisin, sweet spice. Medium tannin, full body.
- **Styles**: mostly rich, full reds, with wines from **old vines** especially prized. There is also pale pink, slightly sweet **White Zinfandel**.
- **Regions**: Sonoma (Dry Creek Valley), Lodi, Paso Robles and Napa (California); Puglia (Italy, Primitivo di Manduria).
- **Food**: barbecue, smoked meats, richly marinated pork ribs.

**Pinotage** was created in 1925 at Stellenbosch University in South Africa by crossing **Pinot Noir** and **Cinsault**. South Africans then called Cinsault "Hermitage", so the two names were combined into Pinotage.

- **Features**: deep colour, medium to high tannin, blackberry and plum, with smoky or earthy notes. Some styles use heavily toasted oak to emphasise coffee and chocolate.
- **Regions**: Stellenbosch and across South Africa. Also used in South Africa's own signature blends.
- **Food**: grilled sausages, barbecue, smoked meats.

</Section>

<Section id="more-and-compare" title="Two more grapes, and a comparison">

Two more regional grapes are well worth knowing.

- **Grüner Veltliner**: Austria's most planted white grape. Green apple, citrus and a distinctive **white pepper** note. High acidity, and a wide range from light everyday wines to concentrated dry wines from the Wachau, Kamptal and Kremstal. We return to it in chapter 24, "Germany, Austria and Eastern Europe".
- **Touriga Nacional**: the key red grape of Portugal's Douro and Dão. Deep colour, high tannin, blackberry and **violet**. The heart of **Port** blends and excellent as a dry red too. We meet it alongside Port in chapter 29, "Sparkling, sweet and fortified".

<Section id="recap" title="Key points">

- **Sangiovese**: the star of Tuscany. High acidity, cherry and dried herbs. Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino (100%), Vino Nobile di Montepulciano.
- **Nebbiolo**: Piedmont. **Pale colour + very high tannin and acidity**, roses and tar. Barolo (at least 38 months), Barbaresco (at least 26 months).
- **Tempranillo**: the face of Spain. Red fruit in harmony with oak. Rioja's categories run **Crianza → Reserva → Gran Reserva**. In Ribera del Duero it is Tinto Fino.
- **Malbec** came from Cahors but blossomed at the high altitudes of **Mendoza**. **Zinfandel = Primitivo**, with roots in Croatia. **Pinotage = Pinot Noir × Cinsault**, born in South Africa.
- Remember **Grüner Veltliner** (Austria, white pepper) and **Touriga Nacional** (Portugal, the heart of Port) too.
