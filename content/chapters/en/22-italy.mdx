<Section id="overview" title="A boot-shaped vineyard">

Italy stretches long from the Alps in the north to Sicily in the south. Almost every region makes wine, and native grapes have been preserved for so long that memorising them all is nearly impossible. So when you start out, you only need to grasp **four areas and their lead grapes**.

The landscape is key. Most of the country is hills and mountains, so even in the hot south, **altitude** and **sea breezes** cool the grapes. As we saw in chapter 9, "Climate and terroir", it is the classic country where you can't judge taste by latitude alone.

Let's recall Italy's quality levels from chapter 13, "Reading a label".

| Category | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| DOCG | The strictest controlled origin + guarantee | Barolo, Brunello di Montalcino, Chianti Classico |
| DOC | Controlled origin | Bolgheri, Valpolicella |
| IGT (IGP) | Regional indication. Grapes and winemaking relatively free | IGT Toscana |
| Vino | Table wine with no stated origin | Everyday wine |

Italian labels sometimes use a **village or region name** (Barolo, Chianti) and sometimes **grape + region** (Barbera d'Asti, Montepulciano d'Abruzzo). "D'" or "di" means "of", so Barbera d'Asti is "Barbera from Asti".

</Section>

<Section id="piedmont" title="Piedmont: fog and Nebbiolo">

**Piedmont (Piemonte)** means "foot of the mountains", a north-western region at the edge of the Alps. Its climate is **continental**: cold winters and hot summers. In autumn, thick fog settles over the Langhe hills, and this fog (nebbia) is the famous supposed origin of the grape name **Nebbiolo**.

| Wine | Grape | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Barolo DOCG | Nebbiolo | Pale garnet, very high tannin and acid, aromas of rose, cherry and tar. At least 38 months' ageing (18 in wood) |
| Barbaresco DOCG | Nebbiolo | A little softer than Barolo and approachable sooner. At least 26 months' ageing (9 in wood) |
| Barbera d'Asti / d'Alba | Barbera | Fruity reds with high acidity and low tannin. Everyday food wines |
| Dolcetto d'Alba | Dolcetto | Soft, fruity, light reds |
| Moscato d'Asti DOCG | Moscato Bianco | About 5% alcohol, sweet and lightly sparkling (frizzante) |
| Gavi DOCG | Cortese | Crisp dry whites |

**Nebbiolo** is a demanding grape that buds late and ripens late. So it takes the best south-facing slopes and leaves the lesser sites to Barbera and Dolcetto. As we saw in chapter 18, "Regional signature grapes", it is pale in colour but very tannic, rough when young, then blooming with rose, dried cherry and tar as it ages.

> Nebbiolo is a wine that looks like weak black tea but tastes like strong green tea. Judge it light by its colour and you're in for a shock.

**Moscato d'Asti** has the opposite appeal. Fermentation is stopped early to keep sweetness and some carbon dioxide is trapped, making a light, sweet, grapey wine. It is a fine example of how "when fermentation stops", which we learned in chapter 1, decides the style.

</Section>

<Section id="tuscany" title="Tuscany: Sangiovese and the Super Tuscans">

**Tuscany (Toscana)** is the central region of Florence and Siena. Its climate is warm and **Mediterranean**, but the hills' altitude cools the nights and preserves acidity. The star is **Sangiovese**, Italy's most planted black grape. It is marked by high acid, medium-plus tannin, and aromas of sour red cherry, tomato leaf and dried herbs.

| Wine | Grape | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Chianti DOCG | Mostly Sangiovese | A wide range from light and fresh to rich |
| Chianti Classico DOCG | At least 80% Sangiovese | The historic heartland. Black rooster emblem. Riserva and Gran Selezione levels |
| Brunello di Montalcino DOCG | 100% Sangiovese | Warmer south, rich and long-lived. Released about five years after harvest |
| Vino Nobile di Montepulciano DOCG | Mostly Sangiovese | A style between Chianti and Brunello |
| Bolgheri DOC | Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and others | Bordeaux-style coastal blends |

Remember that **Chianti** and **Chianti Classico** are different appellations. Classico refers only to the original Chianti area between Florence and Siena.

In the mid-20th century, some producers felt the Chianti rules of the day were holding quality back. So they blended **Cabernet Sauvignon** or **Merlot** into Sangiovese, or made wines solely from international grapes aged in new French oak. Outside the rules, these had to be sold as table wine, the lowest category, but wines like **Sassicaia** from Bolgheri and **Tignanello** from the Chianti area won worldwide fame and earned the nickname **Super Tuscans**. Today most are sold as **IGT Toscana** or Bolgheri DOC. It is a fascinating case of quality running ahead of the rules, turning the classification story of chapter 13 on its head.

</Section>

<Section id="veneto" title="The Veneto: dried grapes and bubbles">

The **Veneto** is the north-eastern region of Venice and Verona. The Alps and Lake Garda moderate a fairly mild **continental** climate. It is one of Italy's largest wine-producing regions, with a wide variety of styles.

| Wine | Grapes | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Valpolicella | Mostly Corvina, plus Rondinella and others | Light, fresh reds with cherry aromas |
| Valpolicella Ripasso | As above | Refermented on the skins left over from Amarone for a richer red |
| Amarone della Valpolicella DOCG | As above (dried grapes) | Rich, high-alcohol dry red with dried fruit and chocolate |
| Recioto della Valpolicella DOCG | As above (dried grapes) | A sweet red made the same way |
| Soave | Garganega | Dry whites with almond and lemon. The Classico zone on volcanic hills is famous |
| Prosecco | Glera | Light, fruity tank-method sparkling wine |

The Veneto's most distinctive technique is **appassimento**. Harvested grapes are spread on racks or in crates in well-ventilated drying rooms and **dried for months**. As water evaporates, sugar, flavour and colour concentrate. Ferment these grapes fully dry and you get **Amarone**; leave sweetness and you get **Recioto**. It is the same principle that makes dried persimmons or raisins so much sweeter and richer than fresh fruit.

**Prosecco** is covered in detail in chapter 29, "Sparkling, sweet and fortified", but remember that its **second fermentation takes place in a large tank**, not in the bottle as in Champagne, to keep the grape's fresh pear and apple aromas. Prosecco from the hills of **Conegliano Valdobbiadene** is DOCG.

</Section>

<Section id="south" title="The south and the islands: sun and volcanoes">

Southern Italy has a hot, sunny **Mediterranean** climate. It was once associated with heavy, rustic bulk wine, but thanks to native grapes and high-altitude vineyards it has become one of the most exciting areas.

| Region | Key grapes | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Puglia | Primitivo, Negroamaro | Ripe black fruit, soft tannin, high alcohol |
| Campania | Aglianico (Taurasi), Fiano, Greco | Long-ageing reds high in tannin and acid, distinctive whites |
| Basilicata | Aglianico (Aglianico del Vulture) | Powerful reds from the slopes of an extinct volcano |
| Sicily (Sicilia) | Nero d'Avola, Nerello Mascalese, Carricante | From fruity reds to the delicate wines of Etna |

Puglia's **Primitivo** is genetically identical to California's **Zinfandel**. If you learned Zinfandel in chapter 18, compare how the same grape looks in southern Italy.

**Aglianico** is so high in tannin and acid that it is called "the Nebbiolo of the south", and it makes age-worthy wines even in the hot south. Sicily's **Nero d'Avola** is the island's signature deep red grape.

The hottest spot right now is **Etna**, the active volcano in eastern Sicily. **Nerello Mascalese** grown on its high volcanic slopes is so high in acid and so delicate it is hard to believe it comes from a southern island, and it is often compared to Pinot Noir or Nebbiolo. It is the perfect place for chapter 9's saying, "altitude beats latitude".

</Section>

<Section id="recap" title="Key points">

- Italy is a land of **native grapes**. The categories run **DOCG → DOC → IGT → Vino**.
- **Piedmont**: continental climate, **Nebbiolo** in Barolo (38 months, 18 in wood) and Barbaresco (26 months, 9 in wood), everyday **Barbera**, and sweet **Moscato d'Asti**.
- **Tuscany**: **Sangiovese** in Chianti Classico and Brunello di Montalcino (100%), and **Super Tuscans** using international grapes.
- **Veneto**: **Amarone** made by **appassimento** with dried grapes, **Soave** from Garganega, **Prosecco** from Glera.
- **The south**: Puglia's **Primitivo** (= Zinfandel), **Aglianico** in Campania and Basilicata, Sicily's **Nero d'Avola** and the high volcanic slopes of **Etna**.
