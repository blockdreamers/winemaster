<Section id="rivers" title="France changes along its rivers">

Follow the rivers and you won't get lost studying French wine. The **Rhône** flowing north to south, the **Loire** flowing east to west, Alsace along the **Rhine** on the German border, and **southern France** facing the Mediterranean. All four have different climates, making them perfect material for reviewing chapter 9, "Climate and terroir".

| Region | Climate | Key grapes | Signature style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Northern Rhône | Moderate continental | Syrah, Viognier | Deep reds with peppery aromas |
| Southern Rhône | Mediterranean | Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre | Rich, warm blended reds |
| Loire | Maritime in the west, more continental eastwards | Sauvignon Blanc, Chenin Blanc, Cabernet Franc, Melon de Bourgogne | High-acid whites, light reds |
| Alsace | Dry, sunny continental | Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris | Aromatic single-variety whites |
| Languedoc-Roussillon, Provence | Mediterranean | Grenache, Syrah, Carignan, Cinsault | Fruity reds, rosé |

</Section>

<Section id="rhone" title="The Rhône: Syrah in the north, Grenache in the south">

The Rhône valley runs from south of Lyon to Avignon, and splits into two areas with completely different characters.

The **northern Rhône** has **steep granite terraces** along a narrow river valley. Reds are made from a single grape, **Syrah**, with pepper, blackberry, violet and, with age, meaty notes. This is the "cool-climate Syrah" we saw in chapter 17, "International red grapes II".

In the **southern Rhône** the river widens, flat land opens out and the climate turns hot and dry, **Mediterranean**. The cold, strong northerly **Mistral** also dries the vineyards and helps prevent disease. Here the standard is the **GSM blend**, built on heat-loving **Grenache** with Syrah and Mourvèdre. Grenache brings strawberry-jam fruit and high alcohol, Syrah colour and structure, Mourvèdre tannin and dark flavours. Most Rhône wine comes from the south.

| Region | Location | Grapes | Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Côte-Rôtie | North | Syrah (sometimes with a little Viognier) | Fragrant, elegant reds |
| Hermitage | North | Syrah / Marsanne and Roussanne for whites | Powerful, long-ageing reds |
| Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph | North | Syrah | Lighter, fairly priced reds |
| Cornas | North | 100% Syrah | Dark, firmly tannic reds |
| Condrieu | North | Viognier | Rich whites with apricot and floral aromas |
| Châteauneuf-du-Pape | South | Mostly Grenache, a dozen or so grapes permitted | Rich, high-alcohol reds |
| Gigondas, Vacqueyras | South | Mostly Grenache | Powerful reds |
| Tavel | South | Grenache, Cinsault | Deep-coloured dry rosé |
| Côtes du Rhône | Whole Rhône (mostly south) | Mostly Grenache | Everyday fruity reds |

**Châteauneuf-du-Pape** takes its name from the "Pope's new castle" built when the popes lived in Avignon in the 14th century. Many of its vineyards are covered with fist-sized rounded stones called **galets roulés**, which soak up the day's heat and release it at night, ripening the grapes fully.

> The northern Rhône is "one grape on steep slopes", the southern Rhône "many grapes on hot stony ground". The same river, but a different grammar of wine.

</Section>

<Section id="loire" title="The Loire: four faces along one river">

The Loire follows France's longest river for hundreds of kilometres, from Nantes on the Atlantic to Sancerre inland. The west is cool and **maritime**, influenced by the sea, becoming more **continental** to the east. With an overall cool climate, it makes many **high-acid whites** and light reds.

| Area (west → east) | Main grapes | Key regions | Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pays Nantais | Melon de Bourgogne | Muscadet | Light, salty dry whites, perfect with oysters |
| Anjou-Saumur | Chenin Blanc, Cabernet Franc | Savennières, Coteaux du Layon, Saumur-Champigny | Dry to botrytised sweet whites, light reds |
| Touraine | Chenin Blanc, Cabernet Franc | Vouvray, Chinon, Bourgueil | Chenin at every sweetness level, herbal reds |
| Centre | Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Noir | Sancerre, Pouilly-Fumé | Crisp, mineral Sauvignon Blanc |

**Muscadet** is made from a neutral grape and can be plain if left alone. So it is aged **sur lie**, resting on its lees through the winter, to add texture and a subtle yeasty flavour. This is the lees ageing from chapter 12, "Oak and ageing", in action.

**Vouvray** is the capital of **Chenin Blanc**, which we met in chapter 15. Its acidity is so high that, depending on the year, it makes dry (sec), off-dry (demi-sec), sweet (moelleux) and sparkling wine. **Chinon** makes **Cabernet Franc** reds with red fruit, pencil shavings and leafy notes. At the eastern end, **Sancerre** and **Pouilly-Fumé**, facing each other across the river, are the world benchmarks for Sauvignon Blanc.

</Section>

<Section id="alsace" title="Alsace: the France that names its grapes">

Alsace lies in a long strip in north-east France between the Rhine and the **Vosges mountains**. Rain clouds from the west are blocked by the Vosges and drop their rain before crossing, so despite its northern latitude it is one of **the driest, sunniest regions in France**. That gives a long autumn in which grapes ripen slowly and fully, aromatic yet still fresh.

Because of a history of passing between Germany and France, its grapes overlap with Germany's, but the style is traditionally **drier and fuller-bodied**. And, unusually for France, it is traditional to **name the grape on the label**. The bottles are the tall, slim **flûte**.

| Grape | Style |
|---|---|
| Riesling | Dry, high in acid, lemon and stony minerality |
| Gewürztraminer | Intense lychee and rose, fairly low acidity, many off-dry |
| Pinot Gris | Rich body, ripe pear and honey, smokiness |
| Muscat | Dry whites that smell like freshly picked grapes |
| Pinot Noir | Almost the only red in Alsace, in a light style |

Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris and Muscat are the so-called **noble grapes**, and wines from the 51 **Alsace Grand Cru** vineyards are in principle made from them. There are also sweet wines such as late-harvested **Vendanges Tardives** and **Sélection de Grains Nobles** from botrytised grapes, and plenty of traditional-method sparkling **Crémant d'Alsace**.

</Section>

<Section id="south" title="Southern France: Languedoc-Roussillon and Provence">

**Languedoc-Roussillon**, facing the Mediterranean, has the largest vineyard area in France. It was once known for cheap bulk wine, but quality and diversity have improved enormously.

- **Reds**: fruity blends of Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre, **Carignan** and **Cinsault**, from Corbières, Minervois, Saint-Chinian and others.
- **IGP Pays d'Oc**: instead of strict AOC rules, international grapes such as Chardonnay and Cabernet Sauvignon are planted freely and the **grape is named on the label**, much like the New World labels in chapter 13, "Reading a label".
- **Whites and sparkling**: high-acid Picpoul de Pinet, and Limoux, famous for traditional-method sparkling wine.
- **Vin Doux Naturel from Roussillon**: fortified wines with grape spirit added during fermentation to keep sweetness, such as Banyuls and Maury.

Most of **Provence**'s production is **pale salmon-pink dry rosé**, made from Grenache, Cinsault, Syrah, Mourvèdre and others with brief skin contact or direct pressing. The rosé winemaking from chapter 11 has become the identity of a whole region. Within Provence, **Bandol** stands out for deep, long-ageing reds made from **Mourvèdre**.

</Section>

<Section id="recap" title="Key points">

- The **northern Rhône** means **Syrah** on steep granite slopes (Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage, Cornas) and **Viognier** (Condrieu).
- The **southern Rhône** means **GSM blends** in a Mediterranean climate. **Châteauneuf-du-Pape** is Grenache-based and famous for its rounded stones.
- The **Loire**, from the west: **Muscadet** (sur lie), **Anjou-Saumur** and **Touraine** (Chenin Blanc, Cabernet Franc, Vouvray, Chinon), **Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé** (Sauvignon Blanc).
- **Alsace** is dry and sunny thanks to the Vosges rain shadow, and **names its grapes on the label**. Grands crus centre on Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris and Muscat.
- **Languedoc-Roussillon**, France's largest vineyard area, offers blended reds and **IGP Pays d'Oc**; **Provence** is synonymous with **dry rosé**.
