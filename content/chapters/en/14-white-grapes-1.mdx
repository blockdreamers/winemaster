<Section id="two-faces" title="A blank canvas and a highlighter">

Part 3, "Grapes you must know", begins with the two pillars of white wine: **Chardonnay** and **Sauvignon Blanc**. Both come from France and are now planted in almost every wine-producing country. But their characters are opposites.

- **Chardonnay is a blank canvas.** Its own aromas are subtle, so where it grew and what the winemaker did are painted on it plainly.
- **Sauvignon Blanc is a highlighter.** Its aromas are so distinct that wherever it is made, it shouts "I'm Sauvignon Blanc".

> Chardonnay is plain steamed rice; Sauvignon Blanc is freshly dressed herbs. Rice tastes different depending on what you eat it with, but fresh herbs smell like themselves wherever you eat them.

In this chapter we look at each grape's character, how its aromas change with climate, the winemaking choices, the classic regions and the food that suits it. Every grape to come will be laid out in the same framework, so get to know it here.

</Section>

<Section id="chardonnay-climate" title="Chardonnay: a face drawn by climate">

Chardonnay comes from Burgundy. It buds early and ripens relatively early. Its strength is adaptability, thriving in cool and warm places alike, which has made it one of the most widely planted white grapes in the world. But budding early also makes it vulnerable to **spring frost**. In northern regions such as Chablis, the battle with frost is repeated every year.

Chardonnay is the best textbook for studying climate. The same grape steps through different fruit aromas as the temperature changes.

| Climate | Aromas | Structure | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cool | Green apple, pear, lemon, wet stone | High acidity, light to medium body | Chablis, Champagne |
| Moderate | Peach, melon, citrus | Medium-plus acidity, medium body | Côte de Beaune, coastal Sonoma |
| Warm | Pineapple, mango, banana | Medium acidity, full body, high alcohol | Napa Valley, inland Australia |

Acidity is higher where it is cooler, while alcohol and body climb where it is warmer. The principles from chapter 9, "Climate and terroir", show up plainly within a single grape.

<Section id="chardonnay-making" title="Chardonnay: winemaking, regions and food">

Chardonnay is called a "winemaker's grape" because it takes well to almost every technique. All the tools from chapter 12, "Oak and ageing", come out here.

- **Stainless steel, no oak**: clean, crisp fruit as it is. The basic Chablis style.
- **Oak fermentation and ageing**: aromas of vanilla, toast and clove, and a rounder texture. The more new oak, the stronger they become.
- **Malolactic conversion**: sharp malic acid turns into soft lactic acid, giving a **buttery** flavour.
- **Lees ageing**: resting on spent yeast adds biscuity, nutty notes and a creamy texture. Stirring the lees (**bâtonnage**) boosts the effect.
- **Sparkling**: a key Champagne grape. Made only from Chardonnay, it becomes **Blanc de Blancs**.

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Chablis (Burgundy) | No oak, or restrained. High acidity, green apple, wet stone |
| Côte de Beaune (Meursault, Puligny-Montrachet and others) | Complexity from oak and lees. Can age a long time |
| Mâconnais (southern Burgundy) | Ripe fruit, easy prices |
| Champagne | Blanc de Blancs; brings finesse to blends |
| Napa Valley and Sonoma (USA) | Generous fruit, from the classic oak-and-butter style to restrained styles |
| Australia (Margaret River, Yarra Valley and others) | A trend towards acidity and precision, led by cool regions |

For **food**, follow the style. Light Chablis suits delicate seafood such as oysters, raw fish and clams; rich Chardonnay with oak and butter suits richer dishes such as pasta in cream sauce, fish pan-fried in butter and chicken. At a Korean table, try it with pan-fried fish fritters or abalone grilled in butter.

</Section>

<Section id="sauvignon-blanc" title="Sauvignon Blanc: grass and tropical fruit">

Sauvignon Blanc is an **aromatic** grape from the Loire and Bordeaux. Its aromas come powerfully from the grape itself, so preserving them is the heart of winemaking. Most is therefore **fermented cool without oak** and made to be drunk fresh.

Sauvignon Blanc's aromas run in two directions.

- **Green**: freshly cut grass, green pepper, asparagus, gooseberry. They come from compounds called pyrazines and are stronger in grapes from cool or shaded conditions.
- **Tropical**: passion fruit, grapefruit, guava. They stand out in grapes that have had plenty of sun.

| Climate | Aromas | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Cool | Grass, green pepper, lime, a flinty minerality | Sancerre, Pouilly-Fumé |
| Cool but very sunny | Grass, passion fruit and grapefruit all bursting out at once | Marlborough |
| Warm | Melon, ripe peach, tropical fruit. Aromas become somewhat muted | Parts of Chile, California |

The structure is much the same everywhere: **high acidity**, light to medium body, medium alcohol, dry. In hot places it easily loses its character, so good Sauvignon Blanc usually comes from cool regions or places cooled by sea breezes.

There are fewer winemaking options than for Chardonnay, but some exist. Top white Bordeaux (from Pessac-Léognan and elsewhere) is fermented and aged in oak for texture and ageing potential, and in California an oaked style is sometimes called **Fumé Blanc**.

</Section>

<Section id="sauvignon-regions" title="Sauvignon Blanc: regions, food, and the two grapes compared">

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Sancerre, Pouilly-Fumé (Loire) | Restrained fruit, high acidity, flinty minerality |
| Marlborough (New Zealand) | Intensely expressive passion fruit, grapefruit and grass |
| Bordeaux | Blended with **Sémillon**. Dry whites and the botrytised sweet wine Sauternes |
| Chile (coastal regions such as Casablanca) | A crisp style thanks to cool sea breezes |
| South Africa | A balance between green notes and tropical fruit |

In Bordeaux, Sauvignon Blanc is more often found with Sémillon than alone. Sauvignon Blanc brings acidity and aroma, Sémillon weight and a soft texture. The same pairing is used in the botrytised wine **Sauternes**. More in chapter 19, "France I: Bordeaux and the South West", and chapter 29, "Sparkling, sweet and fortified".

**Food**: high acidity and green aromas make it a natural with fresh vegetables, herbs and tangy food. Sancerre with goat's cheese is a classic pairing, and Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc is lovely with seafood salads or herb-heavy Southeast Asian dishes. At a Korean table, try it with seafood dishes featuring water parsley or perilla leaves, or refreshing dishes like chilled cucumber soup.

<Section id="recap" title="Key points">

- **Chardonnay** is a subtle "blank canvas" grape. **Climate** and **winemaking choices** (oak, malolactic conversion, lees ageing) decide its taste.
- Chardonnay's aromas move from **green apple and lemon** (cool) to **peach and melon** (moderate) to **pineapple and mango** (warm).
- Classic regions: **Chablis** (no oak, high acidity), **Côte de Beaune** (complexity), **Champagne** (Blanc de Blancs), **Napa Valley** (richness).
- **Sauvignon Blanc** is an intense "highlighter" grape: **grass and green pepper** (cool) and **grapefruit and passion fruit** (sun), high acidity, mostly made without oak.
- Classic regions: **Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé** (restraint and minerality), **Marlborough** (explosive aromas), **Bordeaux** (blended with Sémillon).
