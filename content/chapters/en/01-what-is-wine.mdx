<Section id="definition" title="Wine is made from grapes">

In one sentence, wine is **fermented grape juice**. Ferment apples and you get cider; ferment rice and you get sake. Ferment grapes and you get wine.

Why grapes, though? Because they are close to the perfect fruit for making alcohol.

- **Lots of sugar.** A ripe grape is more than 20% sugar by weight, and sugar is what becomes alcohol.
- **Acidity that stays.** Most fruit loses its sourness as it ripens. Grapes keep a good deal of it, so sweetness and freshness stay in balance.
- **Yeast is already there.** The dusty bloom on grape skins is home to wild yeasts. Crush the grapes and fermentation can start by itself.
- **Plenty of juice.** No water needs to be added.

Almost all the wine we drink is made from one European species, **Vitis vinifera**. Chardonnay, Cabernet Sauvignon and Pinot Noir are all **varieties** of it, the way Fuji, Gala and Granny Smith are varieties of apple.

> Different varieties taste completely different, and the same variety changes again with where it grows. That is why there is always more to learn.

</Section>

<Section id="anatomy" title="Inside a single grape">

A grape has four parts, and each gives something different to the wine.

  <figcaption>A grape in cross-section. A wine's character depends on which parts are extracted, and how much.</figcaption>

| Part | Gives the wine | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | Colour, tannin, aroma compounds | Almost all of a red wine's colour and grip |
| Pulp | Water, sugar, acid | Usually clear, which is why black grapes can make white wine |
| Seeds | Bitter, coarse tannin | Pressing is gentle so seeds don't crack |
| Stems | Green, stalky tannin | Mostly removed, though some producers keep a share on purpose |

The key fact: **grape juice is almost always clear.** Red wine is red because the skins were soaked in the juice. Remove the skins straight away and even black grapes give a clear wine. Champagne's **Blanc de Noirs** is made exactly that way.

</Section>

<Section id="fermentation" title="Fermentation: what yeast does">

Leave crushed grapes alone and yeast feeds on the sugar, giving off **alcohol** and **carbon dioxide**. This is **alcoholic fermentation**.

<figure>
  <Fermentation labels={{ sugar: 'Grape sugar', yeast: 'Yeast', alcohol: 'Alcohol', co2: 'Carbon dioxide', heat: '+ heat' }} />
  <figcaption>The basic equation. Yeast stops when the sugar runs out or the alcohol gets too high.</figcaption>
</figure>

Once you understand fermentation, many wine styles explain themselves.

- **If the yeast eats all the sugar**, the wine is **dry**. Most table wine is dry.
- **If fermentation stops with sugar left**, the wine is **sweet**.
- **If the carbon dioxide is trapped in the bottle**, the wine is **sparkling**.
- **If grape spirit is added during or after fermentation**, the wine is **fortified**, like Port and Sherry.

Roughly 17 g of sugar per litre becomes 1% alcohol. So wines from ripe grapes in warm places tend to be higher in alcohol (14–15%), and wines from cool places lower (11–12.5%). Chapter 9, *Climate and terroir*, explains why.

</Section>

<Section id="structure" title="Five things that shape taste">

When professionals describe a wine, the first thing they look at is not aroma but **structure**. Structure has five parts.

| Element | What it is | How it feels |
|---|---|---|
| Sweetness | Sugar left after fermentation | Sweetness on the tip of the tongue |
| Acidity | The grape's natural acids | Mouth-watering freshness. Think lemon |
| Tannin | Compounds from skins, seeds and oak | Drying, grippy. Like strong black tea |
| Alcohol | Ethanol from fermentation | Warmth at the back of the throat |
| Body | The overall weight of all of the above | Skimmed milk, whole milk, cream |

A good wine keeps these five in **balance**. Too much acid tastes sour, tannin alone tastes harsh, and alcohol alone tastes hot. You will come back to this table with every grape and region you study.

</Section>

<Section id="styles" title="The big map of wine styles">

The world's countless wines fall into three families.

1. **Still wine** – ordinary wine with no bubbles: red, white and rosé.
2. **Sparkling wine** – carbon dioxide dissolved in the wine: Champagne, Prosecco, Cava.
3. **Fortified wine** – spirit added to raise the alcohol: Port, Sherry, Madeira.

Still wines are then sorted by colour, which hints at the variety, skin contact and age.

- **White**: usually white grapes, skins removed quickly.
- **Red**: black grapes fermented with their skins to extract colour and tannin.
- **Rosé**: black grape skins left in the juice for a few hours to a day.

<Section id="recap" title="Key points">

- Wine is **fermented grape juice**, mostly from **Vitis vinifera** varieties.
- Grapes bring **sugar, acid, yeast and juice**: everything alcohol needs.
- **Skins** give colour and tannin; **pulp** gives sugar and acid. Most grape juice is clear.
- Fermentation is **sugar → alcohol + carbon dioxide**. When it stops, and what is added, decides dry, sweet, sparkling or fortified.
- Structure is **sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol and body**. Good wine keeps them in balance.
