<Section id="family" title="The three Bordeaux brothers">

The three grapes in this chapter, **Cabernet Sauvignon**, **Merlot** and **Cabernet Franc**, are all signature Bordeaux varieties. What's more, they really are blood relatives. DNA analysis shows that Cabernet Sauvignon is a natural cross of **Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc**, and Merlot is also an offspring of Cabernet Franc. Cabernet Franc is effectively the parent of the other two.

Each grape has its own role in the Bordeaux blend.

| Grape | Role in the blend | Analogy |
|---|---|---|
| Cabernet Sauvignon | Frame: tannin, acidity, ageing potential, blackcurrant | A building's steel frame |
| Merlot | Flesh: soft fruit, round texture | Walls and insulation |
| Cabernet Franc | Aroma: the freshness of red fruit, flowers and herbs | Windows and lighting |

> Cabernet Sauvignon alone can be firm but angular; Merlot alone can be easy but plain. That is why Bordeaux blends them.

We look at blending again from the region's side in chapter 19, "France I: Bordeaux and the South West". Here we focus on each grape in turn.

</Section>

<Section id="cabernet-sauvignon" title="Cabernet Sauvignon: a firm frame">

Cabernet Sauvignon has **small berries with thick skins**. With little juice relative to skin, it gives deep colour, tannin and flavour. It also **buds late and ripens late**, so it only ripens properly where there is enough heat into the autumn.

**Structure**: deep colour, **high tannin**, high acidity, full body. Astringent and firm when young, but that is exactly what lets it age for a long time.

| Climate | Aromas | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Cool or underripe | Blackcurrant, green pepper, mint, herbs | Bordeaux in a cool year |
| Moderate | Blackcurrant, black cherry, cedar | Left Bank Bordeaux in a good year |
| Warm | Blackberry, black cherry jam, chocolate | Napa Valley, Maipo in Chile |

**Winemaking**: long skin contact extracts plenty of colour and tannin, and most is **aged in oak barrels**. The finer the wine, the higher the share of new oak tends to be, adding vanilla, toast and cedar. With bottle age, aromas of **cedar, tobacco, leather and earth** emerge.

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Bordeaux Left Bank (Médoc, Graves) | The star of the blend. Cedar, graphite, elegant tannins |
| Napa Valley (USA) | Ripe black fruit, full body, new oak |
| Maipo Valley (Chile) | Vivid black fruit, often a minty note |
| Coonawarra, Margaret River (Australia) | Mint and eucalyptus, a well-ordered structure |
| Bolgheri (Tuscany) | The key grape of the "Super Tuscans" |
| Stellenbosch (South Africa) | A style between Old World and New World |

**Food**: strong tannin needs food with protein and fat. Steak, rack of lamb, Korean grilled beef short ribs and aged hard cheeses all work well.

</Section>

<Section id="merlot" title="Merlot: soft, enveloping flesh">

Merlot is the most planted grape in Bordeaux. Its **berries are larger and it ripens earlier** than Cabernet Sauvignon, so it ripens well even in cool clay soils. But because it ripens early, in warm places its sugar climbs quickly and alcohol can run high.

**Structure**: medium to high tannin but with a **soft** grain, medium acidity, medium to full body, medium to high alcohol.

| Climate and style | Aromas | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Cool | Strawberry, red plum, herbs | Bordeaux in a cool year |
| Moderate | Plum, black cherry, fruitcake | Saint-Émilion, Pomerol |
| Warm, late picked | Blackberry, black plum, chocolate. Rich and soft tannins | Napa Valley, Chile, Australia |

**Winemaking**: fruit-forward everyday wines meant for early drinking use no oak or just a little, while top wines gain chocolate, coffee and clove from oak ageing. With age come prune, leather and earth.

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Saint-Émilion, Pomerol (Bordeaux Right Bank) | The star of the blend. Velvety texture |
| All of Bordeaux | Blended into most red Bordeaux |
| Napa Valley, Washington (USA) | Ripe black fruit, richness |
| Chile | Soft, fruity everyday wines |
| Tuscany, Friuli (Italy) | Super Tuscan blends and single-variety wines |

**Food**: its soft tannins go with meat dishes of all kinds. Duck, pork and mushroom dishes, and especially comfortable with sweet-marinated meats like bulgogi and tteokgalbi (Korean grilled patties).

</Section>

<Section id="cabernet-franc" title="Cabernet Franc: the fragrant parent">

As befits the parent of the other two, Cabernet Franc sits between them in character, but it is actually the most fragrant. It has **thinner skins and ripens earlier** than Cabernet Sauvignon, so it does well in cooler places.

**Structure**: medium colour and tannin, **high acidity**, light to medium body.

**Aromas**: **red fruit** such as raspberry and red cherry, **violet**, green notes like bell pepper or leaves, and **pencil shavings (graphite)**. In ripe years the green notes recede and fruit and flowers come forward.

**Winemaking**: in the Loire, many wines are made without oak or in old large casks, to be enjoyed fresh and light, and some single-vineyard wines can age too. In Bordeaux it adds aroma and freshness to the blend. In the Loire it is also used for rosé and sparkling wine (Crémant de Loire).

| Region | Style |
|---|---|
| Chinon, Bourgueil, Saumur-Champigny (Loire) | Single variety. Red fruit, violet, leaves. Fresh |
| Bordeaux Right Bank (Saint-Émilion and others) | Blended with Merlot. Some famous châteaux use a high share |
| Tuscany, Friuli (Italy) | Blends and single-variety wines |
| Canada, eastern US | Cool-climate reds and icewine |

**Food**: with high acidity and light tannin, it is lovely served slightly chilled. It suits boiled pork, roast chicken, grilled vegetables and charcuterie.

</Section>

The trick to telling them apart when tasting is the **grain of the tannin**. Cabernet Sauvignon has firm tannin that grips the whole mouth; Merlot has velvety, soft tannin; Cabernet Franc feels light but lifted by lively acidity. <Section id="recap" title="Key points">

- The three grapes are one family. **Cabernet Franc** is the parent of Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot.
- **Cabernet Sauvignon**: small berries, thick skins, late ripening. High tannin and acidity, **blackcurrant**, and **cedar and tobacco** from oak and age. Bordeaux Left Bank, Napa, Coonawarra, Maipo.
- **Merlot**: early ripening and soft. **Plum, black cherry, chocolate**. Star of the Bordeaux Right Bank (Saint-Émilion, Pomerol) and Bordeaux's most planted grape.
- **Cabernet Franc**: thin-skinned and fragrant. **Raspberry, violet, pencil shavings**, high acidity. **Chinon** in the Loire is the classic.
- The Bordeaux blend combines **frame (Cabernet Sauvignon) + flesh (Merlot) + aroma (Cabernet Franc)**.
