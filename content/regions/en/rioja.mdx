## At a glance

Rioja is a region of Spain's top category (DOCa) in the Ebro basin of northern Spain. Mountains to the north block cold Atlantic winds, giving it a **continental** climate, with warmer Mediterranean influence increasing further east.

## Three subzones

| Subzone | Climate and soil | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Rioja Alta | West, higher and cooler. Clay-limestone | Elegant, age-worthy wines with good acidity |
| Rioja Alavesa | North, cool. Limestone | Delicate and aromatic |
| Rioja Oriental (formerly Baja) | East, warm and dry | More Garnacha, more opulent |

Reds are built around **Tempranillo**, blended with Garnacha, Graciano and Mazuelo; whites are mainly Viura.

## Ageing categories (red)

| Category | Minimum ageing |
|---|---|
| Crianza | 2 years (at least 1 in oak) |
| Reserva | 3 years (at least 1 in oak) |
| Gran Reserva | 5 years (at least 2 in oak) |

Rioja is traditionally known for long ageing in 225-litre **American oak barrels**, giving vanilla and coconut notes. Today there are also many modern styles using French oak and emphasising fruit. Chapter 23 goes into more detail.
