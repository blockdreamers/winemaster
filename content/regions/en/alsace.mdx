## At a glance

Alsace stretches along the eastern foothills of the Vosges mountains in north-east France, on the border with Germany. Rain clouds from the west are blocked by the mountains, so despite its northern latitude it is **one of the driest, sunniest regions in France**. Grapes ripen slowly through a long autumn, giving rich aromas with lively acidity.

## The variety is the label

Alsace wines traditionally **name the grape variety on the label** and come in tall, slender flute bottles.

| Variety | Style |
|---|---|
| Riesling | Dry and high in acidity, with lemon and stony minerality |
| Gewürztraminer | Intense lychee and rose, with fairly low acidity |
| Pinot Gris | Generous body, ripe pear and honey, a smoky edge |
| Muscat | Dry white that smells like freshly picked grapes |
| Pinot Noir | Almost the only red in Alsace, in a light style |

## Grand Cru and sweet wines

The 51 **Alsace Grand Cru** vineyards are, in principle, planted with Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris and Muscat. Alsace is also known for sweet wines such as late-harvest **Vendanges Tardives** and **Sélection de Grains Nobles** from noble-rotted grapes, and for **Crémant d'Alsace**, its traditional-method sparkling wine.
