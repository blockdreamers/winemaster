## At a glance

Sonoma County lies north of San Francisco, between the Pacific coast and Napa Valley. It is larger than Napa and closer to the ocean. Depending on how deep the fog and sea breezes formed over the cold Pacific reach into each valley, the climate changes dramatically within a few dozen kilometres.

## Sonoma's main AVAs

| AVA | Climate | Signature varieties |
|---|---|---|
| Russian River Valley | Cool valley where fog reaches deep | Pinot Noir, Chardonnay |
| Sonoma Coast | Cool zone on the ocean | Pinot Noir, Chardonnay |
| Dry Creek Valley | Warm valley | Zinfandel, Sauvignon Blanc |
| Alexander Valley | Warm inland | Cabernet Sauvignon |
| Carneros (shared with Napa) | Cool zone swept by bay winds | Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, sparkling |

## Sonoma styles

Russian River Valley Pinot Noir is rich in red cherry and raspberry with lively acidity, and its Chardonnay often adds oak's creamy texture to ripe fruit. Dry Creek Valley still has old-vine Zinfandel decades old, producing dense reds with blackberry and spice. Chapter 25 goes into more detail.
