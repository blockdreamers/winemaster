## At a glance

Mendoza is desert country on the eastern foothills of the Andes, far from the sea. Rain is very scarce, so Andean snowmelt is channelled through canals to water the vineyards. The sun is strong and the days are hot, but the vineyards sit at roughly 800–1,500 m, so nights are cool and the **day-night temperature swing** is large. That lets grapes ripen fully while keeping their acidity and aroma. Summer hail and the hot, dry Zonda wind are constant worries.

## Main subregions

| Area | Altitude | Style |
|---|---|---|

## Malbec

Malbec is a grape from Cahors in South-West France that arrived in Argentina in the mid-19th century and flourished in Mendoza. It is deep purple, with plum, blackberry and violet aromas and soft, round tannins. The higher it grows, the more vivid its acidity and aromas. Chapters 18 and 26 go into more detail.
