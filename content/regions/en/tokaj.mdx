## At a glance

Tokaj is an area of volcanic hills in north-east Hungary, near the Slovak border. Where two rivers meet, autumn mornings bring fog over the vineyards, and afternoon sun and wind dry them out. These conditions are perfect for **noble rot (Botrytis cinerea)**, which shrivels the berries into **aszú** grapes concentrated in sugar and acid. Tokaj is also known as one of the first places to demarcate its region and classify its vineyards.

## Varieties

| Variety | Character |
|---|---|
| Furmint | The most important. Very high acidity and readily affected by noble rot |
| Hárslevelű | Adds floral and honeyed aromas |
| Others | Small amounts of Muscat-family grapes and others |

## Tokaj's wines

- **Tokaji Aszú**: hand-picked aszú berries are soaked in base wine or fermenting must to extract sweetness and aroma, then aged in oak. Sweetness used to be expressed by the number of tubs (puttonyos) of aszú grapes added; under current rules the 3 and 4 puttonyos terms have been dropped, and only 5 and 6 puttonyos remain as optional terms.
- **Eszencia**: a rare wine made only from the juice that runs freely from aszú berries, fermented extremely slowly. It is exceptionally sweet and very low in alcohol.
- **Dry Furmint**: a dry white with high acidity and mineral character, which has grown sharply in recent years.
