## At a glance

Champagne lies about an hour and a half east of Paris, quite far north for a wine region. It is so cool that grapes struggle to ripen fully, producing light base wines with high acidity. When that sharp acidity meets bubbles, it becomes delicate Champagne.

## Where do the bubbles come from?

Champagne is made by the **traditional method** (méthode traditionnelle). A dry base wine is bottled with added sugar and yeast and goes through a **second fermentation in the bottle**. The carbon dioxide it produces can't escape, so it dissolves into the wine and becomes the bubbles. It is the fermentation formula from Chapter 1 at work.

## Three varieties

| Variety | Role |
|---|---|
| Chardonnay | Elegance, acidity, lemony aromas |
| Pinot Noir | Structure, red fruit |
| Pinot Meunier | Soft fruit, quicker maturity |

Champagne made only from Chardonnay is called **Blanc de Blancs**; a clear wine made only from red grapes (Pinot Noir and Meunier) is called **Blanc de Noirs**.
