## At a glance

The Willamette Valley stretches north to south in north-western Oregon, between the Coast Range to the west and the Cascade Mountains to the east. The Coast Range blocks some of the Pacific wind and rain, but it is still cool, with plenty of rain in winter and spring. Summers are relatively dry and warm, so the grapes ripen slowly.

## Pinot Noir country

The region began in the 1960s when pioneers looking for a Burgundy-like climate planted Pinot Noir. Today it is America's byword for the grape. Red cherry, raspberry, earth and mushroom weave together delicately, with lively acidity.

## White wines

**Pinot Gris** is Oregon's most produced white, often in a zesty style with pear and apple. Racy Chardonnay has also been highly rated recently. Oregon has stricter labelling rules than the US average: for example, a major variety must make up at least 90% of the wine to appear on the label. Chapter 25 goes into more detail.
