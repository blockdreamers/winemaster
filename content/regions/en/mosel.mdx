## At a glance

The Mosel valley starts near the French and Luxembourg borders and follows the Mosel river until it meets the Rhine at Koblenz. At around 50 degrees north, it is close to the northern limit of grape growing, so nearly all the good vineyards are on **steep, south-facing slopes** that face the sun head-on. The slopes are so steep that most work is done by hand. The valleys of its tributaries, the Saar and the Ruwer, are also part of the Mosel.

## Slate and river

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| Slate | Holds the day's heat and releases it at night, and drains well |
| South-facing slopes | Catch as much of the low-angled sun as possible |
| The Mosel river | Moderates temperature swings and reflects light from its surface onto the slopes |

Thanks to these conditions, Riesling ripens slowly but fully even in the cool north.

## Mosel Riesling styles

Mosel Riesling is feather-light, with green apple, lemon and white flower aromas and high acidity. Off-dry wines at Kabinett or Spätlese level are often around 8% alcohol, and the high acidity balances the sweetness so they taste clean. Dry (trocken) styles have increased recently. With age, honey and petrol notes appear. Villages such as Bernkastel, Piesport and Wehlen are famous. Chapter 24 goes into more detail.
