## At a glance

The Rhône Valley runs north to south along the Rhône river in south-eastern France, but it is really two very different regions. The **Northern Rhône**, with granite terraces along a narrow valley, has a mild continental climate; the **Southern Rhône**, where the river widens and the land flattens, has a hot, dry Mediterranean climate. Most of the wine comes from the south.

## North and south

| Area | Climate and soil | Varieties | Key appellations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Northern Rhône | Continental, steep granite slopes | Syrah / Viognier, Marsanne, Roussanne | Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph, Cornas, Condrieu |
| Southern Rhône | Mediterranean, round stones and limestone | Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre (GSM) | Châteauneuf-du-Pape, Gigondas, Vacqueyras, Tavel, Côtes du Rhône |

Northern reds are made from **Syrah** alone, with pepper, blackberry and violet. The south blends several varieties around **Grenache** to make generous, high-alcohol wines.

## The Mistral and galets roulés

The **Mistral**, a cold, strong north wind, blows often through the Southern Rhône and keeps vineyards dry. Many vineyards in Châteauneuf-du-Pape are covered with round stones called **galets roulés**, which hold the day's heat and release it at night, ripening the grapes fully.
