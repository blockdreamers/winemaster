## At a glance

Bordeaux is a port city in south-west France near the Atlantic, and the vast wine region around it. The Gironde estuary and its tributaries, the **Garonne** and the **Dordogne**, divide the region, and the areas on either side are called the **Left Bank** and the **Right Bank**.

## Why blend?

Bordeaux has a maritime climate, so the weather varies a lot from year to year. Growers plant several varieties that ripen at different times, then raise the share of whichever did well that year. It's a kind of insurance, and an art.

| Area | Soil | Main variety | Signature villages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left Bank (Médoc, Graves) | Gravel | Cabernet Sauvignon | Pauillac, Margaux, Saint-Julien |
| Right Bank | Clay, limestone | Merlot | Saint-Émilion, Pomerol |
| Entre-Deux-Mers | Mixed | Sauvignon Blanc, Semillon | Dry whites |
| Sauternes | Misty riverside | Semillon | Noble-rot sweet wine |

Gravel soils drain well and hold heat, helping late-ripening Cabernet Sauvignon ripen fully. Early-ripening Merlot shines on cooler clay soils.

## The 1855 Classification

The **1855 Classification** ranked the châteaux of the Médoc and Sauternes from first to fifth growth for the Paris Exposition of 1855. Nearly 170 years later it is almost unchanged, and there are only five first-growth red châteaux. Chapter 19 tells the full story.
