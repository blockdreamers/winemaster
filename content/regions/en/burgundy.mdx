## At a glance

Burgundy is a long band of hills in eastern France, running from Dijon towards Lyon. If Bordeaux is about blending, Burgundy almost always uses **a single variety**: Pinot Noir for reds and Chardonnay for whites.

## The vineyard is the name

For centuries, monks recorded the taste of each plot and drew boundaries between them. These parcels are called **climats**, and in 2015 they were added to UNESCO's World Heritage list. Quality levels are assigned vineyard by vineyard.

| Level | Scope | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Grand Cru | Top single vineyard | Romanée-Conti, Montrachet |
| Premier Cru | Superior single vineyard | Meursault 1er Cru Les Perrières |
| Village | One village | Gevrey-Chambertin |
| Regional | All of Burgundy | Bourgogne Rouge / Blanc |

## Main areas

- **Chablis**: the northern tip, with cool, mineral Chardonnay.
- **Côte de Nuits**: the world's greatest Pinot Noir villages.
- **Côte de Beaune**: great whites such as Meursault and Puligny-Montrachet.
- **Côte Chalonnaise and Mâconnais**: good-value everyday Burgundy.
