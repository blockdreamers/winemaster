## At a glance

Marlborough spreads around the town of Blenheim at the north-eastern end of New Zealand's South Island. Serious planting began in the 1970s, and when its Sauvignon Blanc became a global hit in the 1980s, it grew into the region producing most of New Zealand's wine. Mountains to the west block the rain, making it one of the sunniest, driest places in the country.

## Climate and subregions

Days are clear with strong sun, and temperatures drop sharply at night. This big day-night swing creates intense aromas together with sharp acidity.

| Subregion | Character |
|---|---|
| Wairau Valley | The broad central valley. Gravel and alluvial soils |
| Southern Valleys | Hillside valleys to the south. Clay-rich, also good for Pinot Noir |
| Awatere Valley | Further south, cooler and windier. A racier style |

## Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc

Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc leaps out of the glass with fruit like **passion fruit**, grapefruit and **gooseberry**, together with **grassy** notes of fresh-cut grass and green chilli. Most is made unoaked and fresh, to drink young. The contrast with the restrained style of Sancerre from the Loire, made from the same grape, is striking. Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and traditional-method sparkling wine are growing too. Chapters 14 and 27 go into more detail.
