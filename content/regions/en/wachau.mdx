## At a glance

The Wachau is a short stretch about an hour west of Vienna where the Danube runs through a narrow, rocky gorge. Stone-walled **terraced vineyards** climb the steep slopes on the north bank. Warm air from the Pannonian plain to the east meets cold air flowing down from the northern forests at night, so the grapes ripen well while keeping their aroma and acidity.

## Two stars

| Variety | Where it's mainly planted | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Grüner Veltliner | Deep loess and sandy soils near the river | Dry, with green apple, citrus and white pepper |
| Riesling | Poor, rocky slopes higher up | Firm and dry, with peach, citrus and mineral notes |

## The Wachau's own terms

The Wachau growers' association (Vinea Wachau) uses three terms based on the weight of the wine.

| Term | Weight |
|---|---|
| Steinfeder | Light, low-alcohol everyday wine |
| Federspiel | Medium-weight, balanced dry wine |
| Smaragd | Full-bodied, from the ripest grapes, with great ageing potential |

Smaragd is named after a green lizard that lives in the vineyards' stone walls. Chapter 24 goes into more detail.
