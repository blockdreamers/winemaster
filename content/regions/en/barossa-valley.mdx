## At a glance

Barossa Valley is a warm, dry valley about an hour north-east of Adelaide. German immigrants settled and planted vines here in the 19th century, and traces of them remain in village names and food. Phylloxera never reached South Australia, so **old vines** more than 100 years old still bear fruit.

## Barossa Shiraz

Shiraz is the same variety as Syrah from France's Rhône. Under Barossa's warm sun it becomes a red rich in blackberry, plum, chocolate and liquorice, with generous alcohol and body. American oak has traditionally been used a lot, sometimes adding vanilla and coconut.

| Wine | Character |
|---|---|
| Shiraz | Dense, opulent and full-bodied with soft tannins |
| GSM blend | Grenache, Shiraz and Mourvèdre. Red fruit and spice |
| Cabernet Sauvignon | Sometimes blended with Shiraz |

## Neighbouring Eden Valley

The hilly **Eden Valley** east of Barossa sits higher and is much cooler. It is famous for zingy, lime-scented **dry Riesling** and also produces cool-climate Shiraz. The two areas are grouped together in the larger "Barossa" zone. Chapter 27 covers them in detail.
