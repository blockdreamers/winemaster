## At a glance

Maipo Valley spreads around and south of Santiago and is one of the starting points of Chilean wine, where French varieties first took root in the 19th century. It has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and is irrigated with Andean snowmelt. Like the rest of Chile it is free of phylloxera, so many vines are ungrafted.

## Maipo from west to east

| Area | Character |
|---|---|
| Alto Maipo (Andean foothills) | High altitude, cool nights, gravel soils. Chile's top Cabernet region |
| Central Maipo | Warm flatland. Reds with generous fruit |
| Coastal Maipo | Cooled by the ocean. Whites and cool-climate reds |

## Maipo Cabernet Sauvignon

Maipo Cabernet Sauvignon is known for ripe blackcurrant and black cherry with a refreshing lift of **mint or eucalyptus**. The tannins are firm yet smooth, and the best wines are oak-aged and can age for a long time. Blends with Merlot, Carmenère and Syrah are common. Chapter 26 covers it in detail.
