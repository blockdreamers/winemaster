## At a glance

Jerez lies in Andalusia at the south-western tip of Spain, close to the Atlantic. Sherry is aged in the "Sherry triangle" linking three towns: **Jerez de la Frontera**, **Sanlúcar de Barrameda** and **El Puerto de Santa María**. Summers are hot and dry, but humid Atlantic winds temper the heat.

## Albariza and Palomino

The best vineyards are on **albariza**, a dazzling white chalky soil. It soaks up winter rain like a sponge and releases it slowly through the dry summer, helping vines survive the heat. The main variety is neutral **Palomino**, while sweet Sherries use **Pedro Ximénez** and **Moscatel**.

## The main branches of Sherry

| Style | Ageing | Taste |
|---|---|---|
| Fino, Manzanilla | Biological ageing under a film of flor yeast | Pale, dry, almond, bread dough, salty |
| Amontillado | Flor ageing, then oxidative ageing | Amber, nutty, dry |
| Oloroso | Oxidative ageing from the start | Dark brown, walnut and leather, dry |
| Pedro Ximénez | Sweet wine from dried grapes | Very sweet, raisin and molasses |

The principles of flor and the **solera** system are covered in detail in Chapter 29.
